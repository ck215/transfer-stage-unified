Stage DB9 Wiring

Transfer-stage cable: two limit switches, one photoelectric switch and a bipolar stepper on a single DB9, with the controller-side connections the firmware will assume.

Annotations: one-axis validator build (2026-10-09)

Bench check of one stage axis with the stepper-motor-validator firmware: a Teensy 3.5 (identified 2026-10-09) driving one TMC2209 over UART, reading both limit switches and the photo-interrupter. Full pin-by-pin sheet: wiring.html (beside this sheet). The sheet below was drawn for a generic Arduino controller. Where it differs from this build, the blue notes and the Validator build column apply.

  • All nine pins are wired. The firmware reads LS1, LS2 and Vo (Teensy pins 5, 6, 7, internal pull-ups) and uses the switches as an interlock; TEST LIMITS validates them and the photo-interrupter. Centre the axis before each session (50 mm travel, 1 mm lead screw); every MOVE, REVS and TEST REVS is capped at 15 mm.
  • Controller pins differ. The validator uses Teensy pin 2 for STEP, 3 for DIR and 4 for EN, plus TX1 (pin 1) straight to PDN_UART (half-duplex UART; RX1 is not connected). That collides with the D2, D3 and D4 sensor inputs drawn below, so the sensors are on Teensy 5 (LS1), 6 (LS2) and 7 (Vo).
  • Pin 1 takes 5 V from the Teensy 3.5's Vin pin, as this sheet intends. That is safe only because the 3.5's digital pins are 5 V tolerant: on a Teensy 4.x, use 3.3 V. At 5 V the emitter LED runs at about 19 mA through R1 (not the 15 mA quoted); at 3.3 V about 10 mA.
  • The sheet contradicts itself on the limit-switch return. The schematic and caption close LS1 and LS2 to pin 4 (GND), but the pin map says "other contact to pin 5" (Vo). Settle it with a continuity check while pressing each switch: 2 to 4 and 3 to 4, or 2 to 5 and 3 to 5. It matters: with a pin-5 return the firmware sees a switch only while the home slot is clear, because pin 5 is the phototransistor output. The schematic draws both switches open at rest (normally open).
  • Two colour schemes. Motor leads, inside the stage (42BYGH613-01B datasheet): black A, green A−, red B, blue B−. DB9 cable: white A+, grey A−, blue B+, green B−. Blue and green swap roles between the two, so go by DB9 pin number, not colour.
  • Coil check at the DB9, motor unpowered: 6 to 7 and 8 to 9 each read about 1.5 to 2.5 Ω (1.5 Ω coil plus cable and meter leads); 6 or 7 to 8 or 9 reads open; any coil pin to pin 4 or the shell reads open. A closed limit switch reads near 0 Ω, below the coil value. The photo-interrupter LED shows about 1.1 V on diode test, not a resistance.
  • Driver settings for this motor (rated 1.7 A, 1.5 Ω, 3.2 mH): 600 mA rms run (the station value, 35 % of rating), 300 mA hold, CURRENT clamped at 1000 mA, spreadCycle, 115200 baud UART, address 0, Rsense 0.11 Ω. The TMC2209's EN pin is active LOW, like the A4988 and DRV8825.
  • Never plug or unplug the DB9 while VM is powered: breaking a coil under current can destroy the TMC2209.

Decisions the firmware depends on

  • Limit switch contact type. Wire them normally-closed if you can: a broken wire or unplugged DB9 then reads as "at limit" and the motion stops. Firmware reads LOW as safe-to-move and HIGH (contact opened) as triggered. With normally-open contacts the logic flips and a cable fault is invisible.
  • Vo polarity. A bare phototransistor output needs a pull-up (INPUT_PULLUP is enough): beam clear → transistor conducts → LOW; beam blocked → HIGH. Modules with a built-in comparator or Schmitt buffer often invert this, so confirm with a meter on pin 5 while blocking the slot before committing the logic. The symbol beside Vo in the schematic reads as a ground reference, which agrees with a pull-up configuration.
  • 5 V budget. Pin 1 carries the LED (≈15 mA through R1) plus the phototransistor supply; well within an Arduino 5 V pin. Nothing above 5 V is on the cable, so Vo can go straight to D4.
  • Coil pairing. Confirm with an ohmmeter that pins 6–7 and 8–9 are the two coils (a few ohms each, open between coils). Swapping one coil's polarity only reverses direction; mixing coils across pairs stalls the motor.
  • Debounce. Mechanical limit switches bounce for 1–10 ms; debounce in firmware (or a 100 nF capacitor across the input) so a single hit doesn't register as several.
